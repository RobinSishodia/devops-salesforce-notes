For more than four years I worked in operations and process-driven roles. The work taught me a lot about SLAs, escalations, documentation and keeping things running when volumes spike. In July 2022 I decided to move into **Salesforce administration and AWS Cloud/DevOps**, and I've been building towards it ever since.

This post is about how I approached the switch, what worked, and what I'd do differently.

## Why Salesforce and DevOps?

Both fields reward the same habits operations work builds:

- **Process thinking.** Salesforce admins design approval processes, flows and data rules. DevOps engineers design pipelines. Both are about turning a messy manual process into a repeatable one.
- **Attention to detail.** A wrong sharing rule or a missing IAM permission causes the same kind of incident a missed step in an ops runbook does.
- **Working with users.** Admins and support engineers spend a lot of time understanding what people actually need, which is something operations teaches every day.

The two areas also connect. Salesforce DevOps (source control, CI/CD for metadata, tools like Copado and GitHub Actions) sits right between them.

## How I learned

### 1. Certifications as a structured syllabus

I used certifications as a roadmap rather than a goal in themselves. So far I've earned 11, including:

- AWS Certified DevOps Engineer – Professional
- AWS Certified AI Practitioner and Cloud Practitioner
- Salesforce Platform Administrator I and II, Platform App Builder, Agentforce Specialist
- GitHub Foundations, GitHub Actions and Copado AI

Each exam forced me to cover topics I would have skipped on my own.

### 2. Trailhead every day

Trailhead's hands-on challenges check your work in a real org, which is the closest thing to on-the-job practice when you're starting out. I'm now a Double Star Ranger with 250+ badges and 7 superbadges. Superbadges in particular are worth the effort because they read like small real projects.

### 3. Building projects, not just watching videos

Tutorials feel productive, but you only find the gaps when you build something yourself. My most useful project so far is a [Salesforce CI/CD pipeline](https://sishodiarobin.hashnode.dev/building-a-salesforce-ci-cd-pipeline-with-github-actions-and-scratch-orgs) that validates every pull request in a scratch org with GitHub Actions.

### 4. Writing it down

This blog exists because explaining something is the fastest way to find out whether you understand it.

## What I'd tell someone starting today

1. **Pick one track first.** Salesforce admin *or* AWS. Trying to learn everything at once slows you down.
2. **Get one certification early.** It gives you vocabulary and confidence.
3. **Build in public.** A GitHub repo and a blog post about it are worth more than a list of courses.
4. **Use your past experience.** Operations, support, finance and sales backgrounds are an advantage for admin and support roles, not a gap to hide.
5. **Be consistent.** An hour a day beats a weekend binge once a month.
